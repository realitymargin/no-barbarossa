How it works

It's a Monte Carlo simulation: the same war, played out by chance, then counted. The odds are my judgment, written down with what informed each one. No source gives them and nobody can measure them. Read the code.

How the model worksin plain words

It's a Monte Carlo simulation: the same war, played out by chance, a million times, then counted.

  1. It starts in January 1941 with Directive 21 unsigned, so there is no German invasion in 1941. Nothing after that is forced.
  2. It plays forward half a year at a time to the end of 1949. Each half-year it rolls for what people could decide: Malta, Vichy, Spain, Hitler going east after all, Stalin striking first, America coming in, a landing, the bomb.
  3. Strength comes from real numbers. GDP and the share of it at war from Mark Harrison's tables, U-boats from the official German history, oil from the US Strategic Bombing Survey. Those numbers move the odds of what happens next.
  4. One pass is one war. Then we count what came up.

Why it should be doubted, and how it was tested

With every decision pinned to what really happened, the same code has to rebuild the real war, or its tests fail: the front half a year at a time, U-boat tonnage (1942: real 5.49 million tons, model 5.63), Soviet output, German oil. That shows it isn't broken. It doesn't show it's right: it was tuned to that war. Take D-Day out of the real war and it runs a year longer, ending in 1946 against the real 1945.

Germany going east in most wars is an assumption, not a finding. Every historian I read says Hitler wouldn't have held off, so the model makes waiting costly. "Waiting doesn't pay" is a design rule here. Who attacks first is what I put in; what happens next is what comes out.

What it leaves outjets, gas, and the rest
  • Liberators in the East. No run has Germany welcomed. The regime's plan was starvation and deportation, so the model doesn't offer it under Hitler. A successor changing that isn't modelled either.
  • Jets and other wonder weapons. Not modelled as events. Germany's quality falls with years of Eastern war, and at half that rate before it.
  • The French fleet. Not a separate event; Toulon and Mers-el-Kébir aren't modelled.
  • Chemical weapons as a last card. Germany had about 12,000 tons of tabun and never used it. No run does.
  • Roosevelt. One US president throughout. No 1944 election, no death in office.
  • Who holds the plan after Hitler. "Germany attacks" includes the 0.7% of wars where his successor does it.
  • Portugal and the Azores. Not modelled.
  • Stalin and the bomb. His odds of striking don't rise for atomic attack on Germany. They rise with a landing, the US at war and German trouble. That's a real gap, and the video says so.
  • Tanks and aircraft as counts. Strength runs on money and share of output, with quality as a judgment, not on a count of machines. German tank output went from 3.8k in 1941 to 18.3k in 1944 (Harrison), far faster than strength here. So the model can't be checked against tank output, and it doesn't show the Speer boom directly. In this model's real 1944, the sudden fall in German strength is fuel, while tanks and fighters were at their peak.
  • Manpower. Not counted. Germany's trained reserves were nearly gone by 1943 and the USSR's were far larger. All of that sits inside the quality numbers.
  • Rail, food, rubber, tungsten and debt are all one number, supply strain, not each counted in tons. Oil and ship tonnage are the two counted in tons.
  • Seasons and single battles. Half-year turns. The real war's winter and summer swings don't show; it can't stage a Stalingrad. The real front is checked against the map only to the half-year.
  • The Italian front and D-Day's location. A landing is a landing.
  • After 1950. It stops at the end of 1949. Germany still standing then is what's counted, and the stop date moves that number a lot: see the yearly curve.
  • The Holocaust and the occupation's crimes aren't numbers in a war model, and the model doesn't pretend they are. In every ending where the regime survives, they go on.
Questionsthe ones viewers ask
Is this really the model from the video?

Yes. The code is the same file. The browser copy only drops the word export. Run 1,000,000 wars at middle odds and seed 19410622 and you get the same 1.33% holding Europe on any computer.

Where do the odds come from?

They're my judgment, written down with what informed each one. No source gives these numbers and nobody can measure them. Every one is listed here.

Does Germany attacking anyway prove anything?

No. Germany going east in most wars is an assumption I put in, because every historian I read says Hitler wouldn't have held off. The video says so. Forcing neither side to attack is one of the what-if buttons.

My run doesn't match "The video" column exactly. Why?

A different seed changes the dice, and fewer wars means a smaller sample. Middle odds, seed 19410622 and 1,000,000 wars always give the video's 1.33%. The same seed with 100,000 wars gives 1.29%, because it's a smaller sample. Every what-if button except The video's million uses another seed, so those numbers are "about". Small differences are noise, especially for rare outcomes. Same settings, same seed and same number of wars always give the same answer.

Where does the video's "beaten in 94" come from?

It adds the first two rows of the table: defeated after an Allied landing, by the bomb, or asking for terms (72.49%), and defeated by the Red Army alone (21.59%).

The video waves off the real invasion's 2.52 because it's all armistices. Isn't most of the 1.33 armistices too?

Yes, that's a fair point. Of the 1.33, 1.01 comes after an armistice with the USSR. Only 0.32 comes from wars where Germany never fought Stalin at all. The same caution applies to both numbers: neither is a plan. The table shows both rows.

The video takes me into "that 4", then gives 18. What did the 4 get?

Left to run naturally, the 4 wars of 100 where nobody moves end with Germany holding Europe in about 8.4% of them. The 18 is a different run: neither side is ever allowed to attack, in every war. The video says "I forced it".

The video says one assumption keeps it between half a percent and just over 2. My slider went outside that.

That range is for each odd moved between the wider test values listed under "Which odds change the answer", for the declaration 30% to 90%, not the Against and For marks on the slider. The sliders go further on purpose. Setting Hitler's declaration on America to 0% gives about 2.8, and 100% gives about 0.3.

What does the model leave out?

Jets, the French fleet, gas as a last card, single battles, Roosevelt's death, manpower as a count, and any welcome for Germans as liberators. The full list is here.

Does it send my settings anywhere?

The wars run in your browser, and your odds stay there. The page counts visits and which button is pressed with GoatCounter, which uses no cookies and collects no personal data. Its fonts load from Google Fonts, which sees an ordinary web request. Your results aren't sent. If you open a shared settings link, its address, which contains those settings, reaches the counter.

Every decision in the videowhy each person acts

1. Hitler does not sign Directive 21

What just happened: Directive 21 is on his desk in December 1940, after Molotov's talks and with Raeder's Mediterranean plan on the table.

Why this person would do it: there is no good reason to think he would: the premise is ours. Raeder's plan is the real alternative he was shown, and he agreed with its general trend without giving up the East. Source

The other option: sign it, as he really did on 18 December 1940. Why not: this is the change being tested. Every historian I read says he would not have held off, and the video ends on the fact that he signed

forced, 100% of the million

2. Raeder lays out the Mediterranean war to Hitler

What just happened: real event: France has fallen, Britain fights on, and on 26 Sept 1940 Raeder reports alone to Hitler.

Why this person would do it: the navy wanted the Mediterranean cleared before Russia; his report says so. Source

The other option: stay silent and back the army's plan. Why not: the navy's whole war depended on Britain, and Russia gave it nothing

the opening plan is the Mediterranean in about 35 wars of 100

3. Stalin offers to join the pact at a price

What just happened: real event: Molotov's Berlin talks end on 14 Nov 1940, and the note follows on 25 Nov.

Why this person would do it: he wanted Finland, Bulgaria and the Straits, and an agreed sphere toward the Gulf. His own instruction for Molotov lists them. Source

The other option: break off after Berlin. Why not: he wanted to delay war, by the majority view, and the deal still paid him

4. Hitler answers Stalin and takes the deal

What just happened: with no invasion planned, the note is still unanswered on his desk and his grain and oil depend on Stalin.

Why this person would do it: Germany's shortages made Soviet deliveries matter, and Tooze says keeping Stalin as an ally was a real option, at the cost of dependence. Source

The other option: stay silent, as he really did. Why not: in most wars he does: the deal happens in 9 of 100
